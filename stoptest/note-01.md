# Flag Lifecycle Management

Every feature flag moves through a predictable lifecycle, and managing that progression deliberately is the difference between a healthy flag system and an unmaintainable tangle. A flag begins its life at creation, where it should be given a clear name, an owner, a description, and an expected removal date. It then enters active development, where engineers wire it into code paths and toggle it in lower environments. Next comes the rollout phase, where the flag gradually exposes the feature to real users, often through percentage-based targeting. Once the feature is fully released and stable, the flag enters its most dangerous phase: dormancy. A dormant flag that serves one hundred percent of traffic to a single variation is pure technical debt, adding conditional complexity to the codebase without providing any control value. Mature teams enforce lifecycle policies through automation, flagging stale flags in dashboards, opening cleanup tickets automatically, and even failing builds when expired flags remain in code. The final stage is archival, where the flag is removed from code, its configuration is retired, and its history is preserved for audit purposes. Treating lifecycle management as a first-class discipline keeps flag counts sustainable and codebases comprehensible over time.
